Notes

Lifetime chart (bottom graph), stacked components:
- The curve is now a stacked area of its cost components (bränsle/el, skatt, försäkring,
  service, däck, värdeminskning, kapitalkostnad, förväntad haveririsk, leasingavgift),
  with the total as the bold line on top.
- Outside the ownership window the capital cost reflects the chosen financing method
  (except leasing, which only exists during ownership), instead of falling back to cash.

Depreciation model: three ways to pin the curve (in priority order):
1. Restvärde ("Restvärde efter innehavstiden", bottom of Fordon & användning): calibrates the
   ownership window backwards, inköpspris -> restvärde.
2. Nypris ("Nypris", same section, only shown when Ålder vid köp > 0): calibrates the curve to
   the car's OWN history - nypris at age 0 -> inköpspris at the purchase age. Same solver as the
   restvärde, just anchored at age 0; it is the inverse of impliedNewPrice.
3. "Värdeminskningstakt (% av standard)" under Avancerat: a manual multiplier on the whole curve
   (100 = standard, 150 = 50% faster value loss). It feeds the depreciation AND the value the
   haveri-risk total-loss check compares against, since both read the same curve.
Only one of them can drive the single multiplier, so the order above decides. A residual always
wins because it is the amount the owner actually budgets with; the nypris is still used for the
purchase-price verdict below. Overall an empty field (or 0/negative) means "not provided".

Deliberate choice: the solved multiplier scales the WHOLE life curve, including the backward
extrapolation before the purchase. So entering a restvärde or a nypris also moves the estimated
nypris shown for a used car (and the pre-purchase part of the lifetime chart). The alternative -
calibrating only inside the ownership window - would create a kink at the purchase month and make
the lifetime curve inconsistent with the TCO calculation.

Purchase-price verdict (needs BOTH nypris and restvärde, and a car that was not new):
- assessPurchasePrice() fits the curve across the whole life - nypris at age 0 to restvärde at the
  sale - and compares that curve's value at the purchase age with what was actually paid. Under =
  "Bra pris" (less value lost than the model assumes), over = "Högt pris".
- The fair value therefore depends only on the nypris and the residual assumption, never on the
  price paid (so lowering the price flips the verdict rather than moving the benchmark).
- Caveat: the fit composes the backward leg (calendar months before the purchase) and the forward
  leg (mileage-weighted age after it) in one remainingValueFactor evaluation, while the lifetime
  chart's buildLifeValue steps month by month with midpoint rate interpolation. The two can differ
  by a fraction of a percent, which is irrelevant for a percentage-level verdict.
- Because the benchmark uses the restvärde, changing that assumption moves the verdict. That is
  intended ("given that this car was worth nypris when new and X when you sell, what is it worth
  today?"), but it is worth remembering when reading the note.

Depreciation cost is always anchored at the PURCHASE PRICE (you cannot lose money you never paid).
A nypris never becomes the depreciation base - with a 900 000 kr new price and a 300 000 kr
purchase price the calibrated curve is much steeper, but the monthly depreciation stays in the
same order of magnitude as without the nypris (regression-tested). Same rule for the restvärde.

Edge cases (all covered by tests):
- Restvärde above the purchase price, or nypris at/below the purchase price: the calibration
  cannot be expressed (it would need negative depreciation). The manual/standard curve is used,
  resolveDepreciation reports feasible: false, and the note explains which case it is.
- A nypris is ignored for a car bought new (age 0), where it equals the purchase price; the field
  is hidden, and a value left in the input is dropped in readInputs.
- Leasing ignores all three (the leasing company carries the depreciation), which the note says.

Leasing: price assumption, comparison line and the chart
- Inköpspris is hidden while Privatleasing is selected, because it is not known for a leased car.
  The price is instead assumed to be leasingavgift * financing.leasing.priceToFeeRatio (100 =>
  3000 kr/month is a car for about 300 000 kr, as asked for). An entered nypris wins over the
  fee assumption - for a new car it is the same thing, and the ratio is only a proxy.
- resolveVehiclePrice() is the single place that decides the price and reports where it came from
  ("input" / "nypris" / "leasingavgift"), so the chart, the comparison and the notes agree.
- A nypris entered while leasing is used as the PRICE, so assumptionsForMethod() blanks it before
  resolving the curve - otherwise the curve would try to calibrate a history from the price to
  itself and report "infeasible".
- The leasing TCO number itself is unaffected by all of this: leasingCashflow is fee-driven and
  never reads the price, so the assumption only changes the chart and the comparison.

Lifetime chart while leasing: cash purchase as the base, leasing as a dotted line
- The stacked base curve is now a KONTANTKÖP (using the assumed price) instead of zeroing
  depreciation/capital cost/risk inside the window, so the two alternatives can be compared
  directly. Verified: the base is bit-identical to a standalone kontant run at that price.
- leasingMonthlyCost is the dotted line, only inside the ownership window: the fee (plus
  firstPaymentExtra in the first month) plus everything the fee does not include - bränsle/el,
  fordansskatt, försäkring, and service/däck when they are not bundled. The haveri risk is the
  leasing company's, so it is excluded.
- The "leasingavgift" component no longer exists in the stacked curve (the fee is no longer part
  of it); the line carries it instead.

Comparison of the financing methods in the result
- computeTco returns comparison: one row per other method (never the selected one), with the same
  statistic as the headline (risk mean when risk is on, otherwise the deterministic total), so the
  numbers are comparable rather than apples-to-oranges.
- The Monte Carlo is financing-independent (same paths, same value series), so it is simulated ONCE
  in computeTco and reused for every method: only the equity/loan/npv arithmetic is redone per
  method. Measured at 20 years/3000 sims the per-path evaluation costs ~789 ms while the
  simulation itself is only ~58 ms, so simulating per method would have cost ~4x for no gain.
  computeTco(..., { compareMethods: false }) skips the rows entirely (used by the test that the
  comparison does not disturb the primary result).
- Caveat: the leasing row is computed from the leasing fieldset, which is hidden unless leasing is
  selected. Comparing with leasing without selecting it therefore uses the default 4500 kr/month
  fee - select leasing and enter a real offer for a meaningful comparison.

Known caveats / not done:
- Boundary step: for an amortizing billån there is a small upward step at the "Säljs"
  line. Inside the ownership window the capital cost follows the actual (paid-down) loan,
  while outside it assumes a fresh loan at the steady-state financing rate
  (discountRate * kontantinsatsandel + låneränta * finansierad andel). Making it perfectly
  smooth would mean applying that same rate across the whole life, which would also
  slightly raise the in-window capital cost. Not done (left as the current behavior).
- The stacked-area lifetime chart has NOT been visually verified in a browser. It uses the
  standard Chart.js pattern (y.stacked: true + per-dataset stack + fill: true) and the
  underlying data is covered by tests, but a quick manual look in a browser is recommended.

Improvement ideas (recorded after the nypris/restvärde work - none of these are done):

1. Input validation: three paths produce silent garbage today
   - Clearing Innehavstiden gives months = 0 and a headline of NaN (levelMonthlyPayment then
     divides by zero).
   - Clearing "Förväntad livslängd" (expectedLifeYears) gives weibullScaleMonths = 0, and
     weibullHazard then returns Infinity: the result becomes 94% total-loss risk at
     55 435 kr/month against a deterministic 5 925 kr. That field has no `required` attribute
     and Number("") is 0.
   - Letters in Inköpspris pass through parseThousands as 0 and compute quietly (a meaningless
     2 607 kr/month).
   There is no validation layer and no error surface, so the page shows a confident number
   instead of refusing. Fix: a validate(input) pass plus a visible summary, and never render
   NaN/Infinity.

2. Tests do not gate the deploy
   - .github/workflows/pages.yml deploys to Pages on every push to main and never runs the
     suite (96 tests).
   - No package.json, so a fresh clone has no documented way to run them, and `node --test
     tests/` does not work on Node 22 at all - it has to be `node --test tests/*.test.js`.
   - Both real bugs in the restvärde/nypris work were UI wiring rather than math (a stale
     leasing note, and the restvärde note claiming a calibration to "0 kr"). Math tests cannot
     catch those. A DOM-shim smoke test was written ad hoc both times and thrown away; it
     should be permanent, and it would guard the 59 element ids that main.js reads out of
     index.html's 63 (65 getElementById calls in total).

3. The Monte Carlo blocks the UI thread
   - computeTco measures 265 ms for 5 years and 1 228 ms for 20 years with 3000 simulations,
     synchronously inside the submit handler. Phones will be several times slower, and there
     is no spinner and no disabled button.
   - Options: spinner + disabled button (smallest), a Web Worker, or scaling numSimulations
     with the horizon. The lifetime chart itself is cheap (1-4 ms).

4. Works offline, but cannot be used offline
   - All calculation is client-side, which suits the real use case (comparing at a dealership
     with bad signal), but Chart.js is loaded from cdn.jsdelivr.net, so with no network the
     whole page breaks.
   - No manifest and no service worker, so it cannot be installed to the home screen.
   - Fix: vendor Chart.js and add manifest + service worker, i.e. a real offline PWA.

5. A comparison tool that cannot compare or share
   - One configuration at a time: comparing kontant/billån/leasing means changing fields and
     remembering the previous number.
   - No URL state, so a calculation cannot be bookmarked or sent to anyone. The input is
     already a plain JSON assumptions object, so encoding it in the location hash is low-risk,
     and a "pin this result" compare table could then sit on top of it.

6. Modelling gaps that matter for a Swedish buyer
   - Ränteavdrag is missing entirely, i.e. the 30% interest deduction. It moves the
     billån/bolån comparison, which is the main purpose of the tool.
   - Bolån is modelled as interest-only (deliberate, documented) but amorteringskrav is real,
     and the hardcoded 2.6% rate is well below current levels.
   - No escalation: over the lifetime chart's 30+ year horizon, fuel and service are flat in
     nominal terms.
   - Sensitivity and break-even would be nearly free given how pure the calc functions are: a
     tornado chart (±30% fuel, ±10% resale) and "how long must I keep it for kontant to beat
     leasing?".
   - The bonus-malus and tax defaults are assumptions from earlier work, and Swedish rules
     change, so they should be re-verified against current regler rather than trusted.

7. Polish and accessibility
   - The result headline has no aria-live, so a screen reader user gets no announcement after
     Beräkna. The charts have no text alternative (the breakdown table covers the bar chart
     partly; the lifetime chart has none).
   - The field notes are not linked to their inputs (aria-describedby), and there are no
     :focus-visible styles.
   - No dark mode (color-scheme: light is hardcoded) and no print stylesheet - a one-page
     printable summary is the natural artifact for this tool.
   - No favicon, no theme-color, no description or Open Graph tags, so a shared link looks
     bare.
   - The thousands-separator cursor-restore hack and the datalist clear-on-focus workaround
     are fragile on mobile, and the field notes have grown into long dense paragraphs (a short
     label plus <details> would read better).
   - src/ui/main.js is ~500 lines mixing DOM refs, note text and chart config; splitting out
     notes.js/charts.js would help. index.html repeats its <option> lists by hand and could
     build them from assumptions.js instead.
