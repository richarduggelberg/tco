Notes

Lifetime chart (bottom graph), stacked components:
- The curve is now a stacked area of its cost components (bränsle/el, skatt, försäkring,
  service, däck, värdeminskning, kapitalkostnad, förväntad haveririsk, leasingavgift),
  with the total as the bold line on top.
- Outside the ownership window the capital cost reflects the chosen financing method
  (except leasing, which only exists during ownership), instead of falling back to cash.

Depreciation model: three ways to pin the curve (in priority order):
1. Restvärde ("Restvärde efter innehavstiden", bottom of Fordon & användning): calibrates the
   ownership window backwards, inköpspris -> restvärde.
2. Nypris ("Nypris", same section, only shown when Ålder vid köp > 0): calibrates the curve to
   the car's OWN history - nypris at age 0 -> inköpspris at the purchase age. Same solver as the
   restvärde, just anchored at age 0; it is the inverse of impliedNewPrice.
3. "Värdeminskningstakt (% av standard)" under Avancerat: a manual multiplier on the whole curve
   (100 = standard, 150 = 50% faster value loss). It feeds the depreciation AND the value the
   haveri-risk total-loss check compares against, since both read the same curve.
Only one of them can drive the single multiplier, so the order above decides. A residual always
wins because it is the amount the owner actually budgets with; the nypris is still used for the
purchase-price verdict below. Overall an empty field (or 0/negative) means "not provided".

Deliberate choice: the solved multiplier scales the WHOLE life curve, including the backward
extrapolation before the purchase. So entering a restvärde or a nypris also moves the estimated
nypris shown for a used car (and the pre-purchase part of the lifetime chart). The alternative -
calibrating only inside the ownership window - would create a kink at the purchase month and make
the lifetime curve inconsistent with the TCO calculation.

Purchase-price verdict (needs BOTH nypris and restvärde, and a car that was not new):
- assessPurchasePrice() fits the curve across the whole life - nypris at age 0 to restvärde at the
  sale - and compares that curve's value at the purchase age with what was actually paid. Under =
  "Bra pris" (less value lost than the model assumes), over = "Högt pris".
- The fair value therefore depends only on the nypris and the residual assumption, never on the
  price paid (so lowering the price flips the verdict rather than moving the benchmark).
- Caveat: the fit composes the backward leg (calendar months before the purchase) and the forward
  leg (mileage-weighted age after it) in one remainingValueFactor evaluation, while the lifetime
  chart's buildLifeValue steps month by month with midpoint rate interpolation. The two can differ
  by a fraction of a percent, which is irrelevant for a percentage-level verdict.
- Because the benchmark uses the restvärde, changing that assumption moves the verdict. That is
  intended ("given that this car was worth nypris when new and X when you sell, what is it worth
  today?"), but it is worth remembering when reading the note.

Depreciation cost is always anchored at the PURCHASE PRICE (you cannot lose money you never paid).
A nypris never becomes the depreciation base - with a 900 000 kr new price and a 300 000 kr
purchase price the calibrated curve is much steeper, but the monthly depreciation stays in the
same order of magnitude as without the nypris (regression-tested). Same rule for the restvärde.

Edge cases (all covered by tests):
- Restvärde above the purchase price, or nypris at/below the purchase price: the calibration
  cannot be expressed (it would need negative depreciation). The manual/standard curve is used,
  resolveDepreciation reports feasible: false, and the note explains which case it is.
- A nypris is ignored for a car bought new (age 0), where it equals the purchase price; the field
  is hidden, and a value left in the input is dropped in readInputs.
- Leasing ignores all three (the leasing company carries the depreciation), which the note says.

Known caveats / not done:
- Boundary step: for an amortizing billån there is a small upward step at the "Säljs"
  line. Inside the ownership window the capital cost follows the actual (paid-down) loan,
  while outside it assumes a fresh loan at the steady-state financing rate
  (discountRate * kontantinsatsandel + låneränta * finansierad andel). Making it perfectly
  smooth would mean applying that same rate across the whole life, which would also
  slightly raise the in-window capital cost. Not done (left as the current behavior).
- The stacked-area lifetime chart has NOT been visually verified in a browser. It uses the
  standard Chart.js pattern (y.stacked: true + per-dataset stack + fill: true) and the
  underlying data is covered by tests, but a quick manual look in a browser is recommended.
